“Brainrot Police” is a UX/UI redesign of Instagram that I worked on in a five-person team for a university project. Instagram relies on addictive mechanics like the dopamine cycle of unpredictable rewards, infinite scroll, a pull-to-refresh that works like a slot machine, and social validation. These can affect mental health (FOMO, low self-esteem, depression), physical health (eye strain, poor sleep), productivity and real-life relationships. We mapped these “avenues of addiction” and set four goals: make the app less addictive while keeping its essential features, raise awareness of time spent, give people more control, and help them cut down or quit without being forceful.

Our key rule was that every feature had to be backed by scientific literature: we mapped each one to a paper and dropped those we couldn’t support. The result includes a stopping cue instead of infinite scroll, a color-changing time-spent indicator, a grayscale mode, “The Mirror”, an “I’m quitting!” mode and tasks to complete before getting back. I designed the customization features (content filters with hide, warn or show options, the “Friends only” mode, feature toggles and the choice of feed type) and worked on an in-app awareness campaign. The project was graded 30/30.
