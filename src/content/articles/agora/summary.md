Agorà is a system of **smart bulletin boards** that I designed on my own for the Prototyping Interactive Systems course of my MSc in Human-Computer Interaction (presented in December 2025). It starts from a simple question: how do you build a community in a university, and actually reach people with events and news?

Traditional bulletin boards get ignored, rarely change and don’t call for action, so I tried to make them smarter with three touchpoints (a kiosk, an app and a website) that work as one system. The kiosk is a multi-view, multi-function station (main and secondary screen, proximity and NFC sensors, a slot to scan paper flyers) that keeps moving until someone approaches, with navigation in a central app bar designed for one main user. Posts can be added from a USB stick or by scanning a flyer (AI extracts the information), or manually, then filtered, viewed “clean” or “messy”, shared via QR, e-mail or NFC, and moderated by AI and humans. The same boards live in the university app and on a website, and the kiosk also links to Cicerone navigation, timetables, doodling and games. Looking back, what I would work on next is designing beyond the happy flow, adding an offline mode and exploring multi-user interaction.
