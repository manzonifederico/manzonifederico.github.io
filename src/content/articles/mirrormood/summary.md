MirrorMood is a research proposal Michele Cazzaro and I wrote for the Affective Computing course of our MSc in Human-Computer Interaction. Smart mirrors that recognize emotions already exist, but they focus on functionality and offer no guidance on how to feel empathetic without feeling like surveillance. We designed an affect-aware mirror that reads facial expressions and voice, places the user’s emotional state on Russell’s circumplex with two transparent logistic regressions, and responds with light, music, words and short guided exercises, reinforcing positive states and never amplifying distress. Only derived features are ever stored.

The study asks when such a mirror feels **empathetic rather than intrusive**, and whether that changes from room to room. It combines a 2×2 lab experiment (80 participants: face-only vs. multimodal sensing, aware vs. not aware of what is sensed) with a 12-day in-home deployment across bathroom, bedroom and living room (30 participants), using validated questionnaires, logs, diaries and interviews. The study hasn’t been run yet; its expected outcome is a set of design guidelines for empathetic ambient systems.
