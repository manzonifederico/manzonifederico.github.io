Games put a great deal of effort into teaching brand-new players, but far less is known about how they welcome players back. Re-onboarding is the study of my Master’s thesis, which I’m running at the University of Waterloo (Stratford School of Interaction Design and Business and the Games Institute), and it looks at exactly that moment: coming back to a video game after a long break.

We want to understand what players go through when they return to a game they stopped playing at least a month ago: what they remember, what confuses them, the frictions they run into and how they cope. And from there, how user-centred design could better support returning players, which is what we call “re-onboarding”.

To find out, participants go back to a game from their own backlog, at home, on their own device, for an hour of play, briefly noting what stands out along the way. Then they tell us about it, in a questionnaire right after playing and in an interview a day or two later.

The study is still running: the results will follow once it’s done.
